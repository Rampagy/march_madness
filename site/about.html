The numbers behind the bracket

A small experiment in big bracket odds.

March Madness Bracket Challenge generates and scores large batches of tournament brackets. The site gives you a look at how those simulated brackets fared against the games as they were played.

Explore the current dashboard

How it works

01

Estimate each matchup

The generator uses observed outcomes from past tournament brackets for seed matchups when there is data. For matchups without enough history, a logistic model based on the seed difference fills in.

02

Simulate the tournament

It samples a winner for each game using those matchup probabilities, then advances the winners round by round to create a complete bracket.

03

Compare the results

Brackets are scored against the season’s recorded results. The dashboard shows the score distribution, leading brackets, and how many games have been decided.

Small brackets, big counts

A bracket has 63 games, but storing all 63 winning team numbers is unnecessary. Each game is a choice between two teams, so the current format stores that choice as one bit: zero for the first team in the matchup, one for the second.

63 game outcomes

That is the size of the 2026 archive in this project, not a promise that every season produces the same volume. The generator splits output after every 120 million brackets, so those 2026 records occupy 104 binary files. The former text and one-byte-per-pick formats are easier to inspect, but need much more space.

Reading the scores

Points double each round: 1 for a first-round game, then 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32 for the championship. A perfect bracket across all 63 games earns 192 points. While a tournament is underway, the maximum possible score reflects only games that have results so far.

These are simulations based on historical seed outcomes, not guarantees of what will happen in a tournament.
